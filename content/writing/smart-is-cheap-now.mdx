I've been thinking about this a lot lately. Every day I watch an agent do in ten minutes what used to take me a full afternoon. Sometimes better than me. And the honest first feeling is not "wow". It's a small sting.

That sting is ego. And I think a lot of smart people are going to feel it very hard in the next few years.

## Being smart used to be rare

For most of history, intelligence was scarce. If you were the one who could read the contract, fix the server, do the math, write the code, you were valuable. People needed you. So it's normal that a lot of smart people built their identity on it. "I'm the smart one" is a very comfortable place to live.

I know this place well. At 12 I was coding without knowing it was coding. At 18 I was building a 3D engine with raytracing because I wanted to do everything myself. Pure ego (haha). But it worked. Being able to figure things out was my thing.

## Now it's a consumable

![Intelligence, now on a monthly plan](/images/ego/subscription.svg)

Today you can buy a very good brain by the token. It doesn't sleep, it doesn't get tired, it doesn't need to be convinced. It costs less than a coffee per task. Intelligence is turning into electricity: you don't brag about having electricity, you just plug things in.

So if your ego is "I know more than everyone in the room", you have a problem. Because the room now has a laptop in it. And the laptop knows more.

What I see a lot is people reacting in two ways. Some refuse to use it, because using it feels like admitting it's as good as them. Others use it in secret and still take the credit. Both come from the same place. The ego is attached to the wrong thing.

## We already watched it happen three times

### Artists, since gen AI

![2022: one sentence in, an image out twenty seconds later](/images/ego/artists-prompt.svg)

Artists got hit first. In 2022 Midjourney and Stable Diffusion showed up, and suddenly anyone could type a sentence and get an image that looked like ten years of practice. ArtStation got flooded with "No AI" protest images. A Midjourney picture won a fine art competition at the Colorado State Fair, and people were furious.

A lot of that anger was fair (their work was used to train the models without asking). But part of it was also ego. "I can draw and you can't" stopped being a moat overnight.

### Developers, in 2024

![2024: asking for a whole feature, not a line of autocomplete](/images/ego/developers-2024.svg)

Then it was our turn. 2024 is when coding models started to be actually good. Claude 3.5 Sonnet came out, Cursor took off, and you could ask for a full feature instead of a line of autocomplete. I moved to Cursor that year myself.

I remember how a lot of devs reacted. "It writes bugs." "It's just a fancy autocomplete." "Real engineers don't need it." Some of it was true at the time. But a lot of it was people protecting the thing they were proud of. Being the one who can make the computer do things was our whole identity. Two years later, most of the people who said that use agents every day.

### Mathematicians, this month

![2026: thousands of agents, a proof checked in Lean, 700+ manuscripts in one day](/images/ego/mathematicians-2026.svg)

And now it's math. Math was supposed to be the last place. The purest kind of intelligence, the thing only a few humans on earth can do.

In September, OpenAI announced that around 10,000 agents running for 88 hours had proved a Navier-Stokes blowup, one of the Millennium Prize problems, checked in Lean. On October 6 they dropped more than 700 math manuscripts at once, all from a model nobody outside can use. Some of them claim results on huge open problems.

A group of mathematicians answered with a statement: "Mathematicians did not ask for this work to be done." And: "Releasing over 700 files at once is not a demonstration of scholarship, but a demonstration of power."

They have real points (some papers were already retracted, and nobody can check 700 files in a week). But the quote that stuck with me is from Luis Martínez-Zoroa, who had been working on the same problem for years: "It would have been nice to do this ourselves."

That one sentence is the whole article. It's not about whether the proof is right. It's about who gets to be the one who found it.

## An aparté about art

![Same painting. A 5 year old or a model. Neither one makes it art.](/images/ego/art-origin.svg)

Funny thing though. I think art is paradoxically the field most protected from AI. Even though artists got hit first.

Because a huge part of the value of art comes from its origin. Who made it, why, in what moment of their life. The object alone was never enough.

A 5 year old can paint an abstract painting. It can even look a lot like a real one. It doesn't give it the status of art. That was true way before AI.

Replace the 5 year old by a model and that's it. The model can make the image. It can't be the origin.

Code and math don't work like that. Nobody cares who wrote the function if it works. A proof is true or false no matter who found it. That's exactly why the developers and the mathematicians should be more worried than the painters.

## What stays scarce

When something becomes cheap, the value moves to what's around it. Here's what I think stays rare:

- **Knowing what to ask.** The model answers everything. Most people still don't know what the right question is.
- **Taste.** Ten good options in thirty seconds is easy now. Picking the right one is not.
- **Owning the result.** The agent doesn't get fired when it ships the wrong thing. You do. Being the one who takes responsibility is worth more than ever.
- **Being nice to work with.** Nobody needs you for your IQ anymore. They need you because they like building with you.
- **Curiosity.** The people who win are the ones who play with the tool, not the ones who defend against it.

None of this is "intelligence" in the old sense. It's more like judgment, and character.

## Move the ego, don't kill it

I don't think the answer is "be humble and forget about ego". Ego is what makes you want to build something great. Without it I would never have opened the Minecraft files in the first place.

The answer is to move it. Stop being proud of being the smartest one. Be proud of what you decide, what you ship, and who you build with. That's the part the subscription can't do for you.

The smartest person in the room is a subscription now. Fine. Someone still has to decide what the room is for.
