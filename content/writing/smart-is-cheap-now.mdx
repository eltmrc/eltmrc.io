For most of human history, **chess** was *the* proof you were smart. Good at chess = genius. That was basically the deal.

Then **1997**. Deep Blue beats **Garry Kasparov**, world champion. Ask Kasparov how that felt (he accused IBM of cheating, he just couldn't believe a machine played like that). Today a free app on your phone beats every human alive. Not some superintelligence. Glorified algorithms.

And honestly the funny part is chess didn't die. More people play it today than ever. People just stopped thinking that being good at chess made you the smartest person on earth.

I think the same thing is happening right now. But to intelligence itself.

## Being "the smart one" was a whole personality

Intelligence used to be rare. If you could read the contract, fix the server, do the math, write the code, people needed you.

So a lot of people built their whole identity on it. And it's not even a bad thing, it's very human. "I'm the smart one" is a really comfortable place to live.

## Now it's on a monthly plan

![Intelligence, now on a monthly plan](/images/ego/subscription.svg)

Today you can buy a very good brain by the token. It doesn't sleep, doesn't get tired, doesn't need to be convinced. It costs less than a coffee per task. It's becoming like electricity: you don't brag about having electricity, you just plug things in.

So if your ego is "I know more than everyone in the room", well. The room has a laptop in it now. And the laptop knows more.

What I see a lot is two reactions. Some people refuse to use it, because using it kinda feels like admitting it's as good as them. Others use it in secret and still take the credit. Same root. The ego is just attached to the wrong thing.

## It already happened three times

### Artists · since 2022

![2022: one sentence in, an image out twenty seconds later](/images/ego/artists-prompt.svg)

Artists got hit first. **2022**, Midjourney and Stable Diffusion show up, and suddenly anyone types one sentence and gets an image that looks like ten years of practice. ArtStation got flooded with "No AI" protest images. A Midjourney picture won a fine art competition at the Colorado State Fair, and people were furious.

A lot of that anger was fair (their work was used to train the models without anyone asking). But part of it was also ego. "I can draw and you can't" stopped being a moat overnight.

### Developers · 2024

![2024: asking for a whole feature, not a line of autocomplete](/images/ego/developers-2024.svg)

Then developers. **2024** is when coding models started to be actually good. **Claude 3.5 Sonnet** came out, **Cursor** took off, and you could ask for a full feature instead of one line of autocomplete. I moved to Cursor that year too.

The reactions were always the same. "It writes bugs." "It's just fancy autocomplete." "Real engineers don't need it." Some of it was true at the time, honestly. But a lot of it was people protecting the thing they were proud of. For a lot of devs, being the one who makes the computer do things *was* the identity.

Two years later, most of them use agents every day.

### Mathematicians · this month

![2026: thousands of agents, a proof checked in Lean, 700+ manuscripts in one day](/images/ego/mathematicians-2026.svg)

And now math. Math was supposed to be the last place. The purest kind of intelligence, the thing only a handful of humans on earth can do.

In **September**, OpenAI announced that around **10,000 agents** running for **88 hours** had proved a Navier-Stokes blowup, one of the Millennium Prize problems, checked in Lean. Then on **October 6** they dropped **700+ math manuscripts** at once, all from a model nobody outside can use.

A group of mathematicians answered with a statement:

> "Mathematicians did not ask for this work to be done." And: "Releasing over 700 files at once is not a demonstration of scholarship, but a demonstration of power."

They have real points (some papers already got retracted, and nobody can check 700 files in a week). But the line that stuck with me is from **Luis Martínez-Zoroa**, who had worked on the same problem for years:

> "It would have been nice to do this ourselves."

That's basically the whole article in one sentence. It's not about whether the proof is right. It's about who gets to be the one who found it.

## Quick aparté on art

![Same painting. A 5 year old or a model. Neither one makes it art.](/images/ego/art-origin.svg)

Funny thing tho. I think art is paradoxically the field the *most* protected from AI. Even if artists got hit first.

Because a huge part of art's value comes from its origin. Who made it, why, at what moment of their life. The object alone was never enough.

Like, a 5 year old can paint an abstract painting. It can even look a lot like a real one. Nobody gives it the status of art. That was true way before AI.

Replace the 5 year old by a model, and that's it.

Code and math don't work like that. Nobody cares who wrote the function if it works. A proof is true or false no matter who found it. So honestly, I think developers and mathematicians should be way more worried than painters.

## So what stays rare?

When something gets cheap, the value moves to what's around it. My list:

- **Knowing what to ask.** The model answers everything. Most people still don't know what the right question is.
- **Taste.** Getting ten good options in thirty seconds is easy now. Picking the right one, not so much.
- **Owning the result.** The agent doesn't get fired when it ships the wrong thing. You do.
- **Being nice to work with.** Nobody needs you for your IQ anymore. They need you because they like building with you.
- **Curiosity.** The people who win are the ones playing with the tool, not the ones defending against it.

None of this is "intelligence" in the old sense. It's more judgment, and character.

## Move the ego, don't kill it

I don't think the answer is "be humble and forget about ego". Ego is also what makes people want to build great things. Killing it would be a waste.

I think the answer is to move it. Stop being proud of being the smartest one. Be proud of what you decide, what you ship, and who you ship it with.

Chess players figured that out almost 30 years ago. The rest of us are just catching up.
